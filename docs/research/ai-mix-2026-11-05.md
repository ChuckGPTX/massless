# Automatic Mixing Rulebook (research notes for the in-browser AI mixer)

Compiled 2026-11-05. Every number carries a source tag `[n]` from the table in section 1.

**Tag conventions**
- `[E]` marks an engineering default chosen inside the cited ranges where no source gives one exact value. Treat `[E]` values as tunable defaults, not facts.
- "LU" means loudness units relative to something else. "LUFS" means absolute integrated loudness.
- "GR" means gain reduction.
- Where sources disagree, the range is given and the conflict is noted.

## 2. Processing order (consensus)

### 2.1 Global workflow
The order sources agree on is [1][54][4][10]:
1. Clean up and edit.
2. Gain stage.
3. Static fader balance.
4. Corrective EQ and HPF.
5. Dynamics.
6. Tone EQ and saturation.
7. Send effects.
8. Buses.
9. Mix bus and master.

Supporting details:
- Balance is the dominant factor, "~75% of the record" [1].
- Several pros set up the mix bus at the start and mix into it [4][10][11].
- In the browser, apply the mix-bus chain from the first render so per-track decisions are made "through" it [4][10].

**Gain staging targets before processing**
- Vocal RMS −18 to −14 dBFS, peaks ≤ −6 dBFS [37].
- 808 starts around −18 dBFS [35].
- Keep about 6 dB of headroom on the mix bus [33].
- Backing vocals can be loudness-normalized to −23 LUFS first [49].
- The ML papers pre-normalize stems to a per-stem-type average loudness and spectrum before mixing [30]. That is a good first step for our pipeline.

### 2.2 Per-track channel strip (recommended Web Audio order)
The order follows the DMC strip [28] and Martínez-Ramírez's EQ, DRC, pan, loudness ordering [30]:

`input trim → (polarity/phase align) → HPF/LPF → corrective EQ (cuts) → compressor 1 (fast/peak) → de-esser* → compressor 2 (slow/opto) → tone EQ (boosts) → saturation → fader/gain-rider → pan/width → sends (pre-reverb EQ) → bus`

- Corrective EQ comes before compression. A boomy vocal's HPF also goes before the compressor [54].
- \*De-esser placement varies. After the compressor, because compression raises sibilance [61][37]. Or after both compressors [54]. Default: between compressors 1 and 2 [E]. Pre-compression clip-gain edits of the esses also help [49].
- Saturation goes after compression and before additive EQ [37].
- Pitch correction comes first in [2][54] but after de-essing in [37]. Default: tune first, because the tracker sees an unprocessed signal [E]. Retune speeds are in section 3.

### 2.3 Lead vocal chains (reference)
- **Hip-hop (pro session)** [2]: low cut → Auto-Tune → 1176 → sibilance-only dynamics → 4-band multiband (low mids and upper mids) → EQ restoring the top → air → slap and delay → resonance suppressor → parallel 1176 at 20%.
- **Trap** [37]: gate → subtractive EQ → compressor → de-esser → pitch correction → saturation → additive EQ/air → short reverb → delay.
- **Sound On Sound, Lil Tecca** [38]: EQ → compressor → channel strip → two de-essers → gate → distortion → peak catcher (C4). Tuning is used as an instrument.

### 2.4 Bus structure
- **Drum bus:** kick, snare, hats and percussion. Optionally include the 808 so it is clipped together with the drums [2].
- **Beat or music bus:** music stems, or the 2-track beat [4][38].
- **Vocal bus:** lead, doubles, ad-libs and BVs.
- **FX returns:** 100% wet [54][56][38].
- **Mix bus.**

Mix bus chain seen in the sources:
- Saturation → clip [38].
- Glue compressor (vari-mu or SSL-style) [11][41].
- Tonal EQ [36].
- Clipper → limiter [2][36].

## 3. Per-role rules

**Levels convention.** Levels are relative to the lead vocal unless noted, as a steady-state LU/dB difference measured on the stem's loudness in sections where both play.
- Anchor 1: the lead vocal ≈ −2.7 ±1.6 LU relative to the full mix [26].
- Anchor 2: the vocal ≈ the loudness of the sum of everything else, and 3–8 LU above any single element [25].

### 3.1 Kick
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Level | −13.2 ±4.1 LU vs mix [26], so about −10 dB vs vocal [26]. In hip-hop and EDM the kick plus 808 should dominate the low end, with the kick as the loudest low-end element [64]. |
| HPF | 30 Hz at 18 dB/oct [64]; 30–40 Hz [70]. In trap with an 808, cut deep sub: −12 dB at 30 Hz [38]. No HPF in KEAMS [24]. |
| Cuts | Box at 600–700 Hz [64]. Mud/hollow at ~400 Hz [64][72]. −4 dB at 4.2 kHz if clicky [39]. LPF ~16 kHz [64]; ~3 kHz for vintage tone [39]. |
| Boosts | Thump at ~120 Hz, carefully [64]. Bottom at 50–60 Hz [72]; fundamental ~65 Hz [45]. Beater at 3–5 kHz [72], 4 kHz [64], or 6–7 kHz [34]. API-style +500 Hz and +5 kHz [34]. Gain ±3 dB default [24]. |
| Compressor | Range: ratio 4–4.6:1, attack 3–4 ms [64] to 50 ms [24], release 150–200 ms [64] to 1000 ms [24], GR 3–4 dB (aggressive 5–6:1 at 6–8 dB) [64], serial. Sources disagree on attack: short (3–4 ms) shapes the punch, long (50 ms) passes the transient. Default 4:1, 10 ms, 150 ms, 3–4 dB GR [E]. Trap uses the "fastest 1176" for transient grit [36]. |
| Saturation | Subtle console or tape [38]. Inflator or soft clip in trap [36]. |
| Pan | Centre, mono [25][64]. |
| Reverb | Usually none. Drums use short pre-delay (5–50 ms) if any [59]. A 55 ms slapback is a stylistic option [39]. |
| Sidechain | The kick is the trigger: it ducks the 808 and bass [2][33][63], and in EDM optionally the pads and synths [63]. |

### 3.2 Snare / clap
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Level | −16.8 ±6.2 LU vs mix [26], so about −14 dB vs vocal [26]. The snare's backbeat must survive when the vocal enters. Reggaeton keeps snares loud [74]. |
| HPF | 80–100 Hz, up to 120–150 if the sample has enough low end [70]. Start at 30 Hz with 12 dB/oct and raise to taste [68]. |
| Cuts | Ring or box at 400–900 Hz as needed [72]. |
| Boosts | Body at 100–240 Hz [68][72]. Attack 900 Hz–5 kHz [68][72]. Crisp at 5 kHz, snap/sizzle at 10 kHz [68][72]. Reggaeton: boxy +200–400 Hz [74]. |
| Compressor | 4:1, slow attack, 2–6 dB GR [68]. Default attack 20 ms, release 100 ms [E]. Trap: fast 1176 [36]. |
| Saturation | Bit-crush or detune for reggaeton boxiness [74]. |
| Pan | Centre [25]. Reggaeton doubles are detuned a few cents and panned L/R, checked in mono [74]. |
| Reverb | Short reverb on snare and hats [36]. Plate on claps with low mix [2]. Pre-delay 5–50 ms [59]. Default 0.8–1.2 s plate, send −15 dB [E]. |
| Sidechain | Snare keys a dynamic cut at 300–500 Hz in pads [67]. Snare can key a subtle 1–2 dB duck at 4:1 on music [63]. Snare-triggered vocal compression or multiband helps the vocal sit "in" the beat [11]. |

| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Level | Part of "other drums," −12.7 ±5.5 LU vs mix together with percussion [26]. Default hats −14 to −18 dB vs vocal [E]. |
| HPF | ~300 Hz typical [66]; up to 400–500 Hz [70]. Exception: a hat may keep its low end as a percussive element [38]. |
| Cuts | Clang at ~200 Hz [72]. Harsh at ~6 kHz in the 2-track case [2]. Resonances at 4–8 kHz [66]. |

| Reverb | Short reverb [36], a "splash" [66]. |
| Sidechain | None. |

### 3.4 Percussion (shakers, congas, rims, log-drum tops)
- **Level:** shares the "other drums" budget [26]. In afrobeats the vocal must sit above the dense shakers, hats and log drum [61].
- **HPF:** 200 Hz+ for small percussion [E, cf. 70]. Conga ring at 200 Hz [72].
- **EQ:** cut ring at ~200 Hz; slap at ~5 kHz [72].
- **Compressor:** fast 1176-style for trap aggression [36]. Otherwise 3–4:1 at 2–4 dB [E].
- **Pan:** spread wide, about 1/3 of all elements panned wide [25]. Sample-delay Haas tricks push percussion to the far sides [36].
- **Reggaeton:** keep percussion short and gated [74].

### 3.5 808 / sub bass
| Parameter | Value |
|---|---|
| Level | Start around −18 dBFS [35]. Hip-hop's LTAS has the most extended low end [27]. A measured 808 track ran at −6 LUFS with an 808 at ~46 Hz [22]. Default 808 ≈ −3 to −6 LU vs vocal in trap [E]. |
| Key | Tune to the song root, or a fifth above [35]. |
| HPF | ~20–25 Hz to remove DC and inaudible energy [E, cf. 70 bass 25–35]. |
| Cuts | Gentle cut at ~250 Hz [35]. A dynamic notch at the kick fundamental (example 62 Hz), keyed by the kick [33]. Cut the 808's lows where they overlap the kick [36]. |
| Boosts | Near the root (~40–50 Hz) [33][35]. +3 kHz "fuzz" for small-speaker audibility [38]. Shelf at 8 kHz only if there is real top content [35]. LPF 48 dB/oct at 6.5 kHz: "fuzz, not hair" [38]. |
| Compressor | Fast attack and release to shape [35][36]. Default 4:1, 5 ms, 80 ms, 3–4 dB GR [E]. |
| Saturation | Essential for audibility on phones [36][38]. Tube at 30% wet [33]. Parallel distortion [35]. Harmonics on a bus [2]. Band-limit parallel distortion to 250 Hz–5 kHz so the sub stays clean [39]. |
| Pan | Mono. Sub mono below 80 Hz [64]. Content below 200 Hz centred [25]. |
| Reverb / delay | None. Never send lows to reverb [25]. |
| Sidechain | Ducked by the kick on every hit [2][33][36]. Full-band: threshold −30 dB, range −10 dB [34]. Or dynamic EQ at the kick fundamental (~62–65 Hz), Q 1–2, 5–10 dB [33][45]. Kick-to-bass compressor at 5:1, 4 ms, 60 ms [63]. Flip polarity or nudge if kick and 808 cancel [38][64]. |

### 3.6 Bass guitar / synth bass (non-808)
- **Level:** ≈ −9.5 LU vs mix (from the ISMIR table, alignment uncertain), so about −7 dB vs vocal [26]. Bass has the lowest crest factor of any stem (2.57 at 100 ms) [26], so control it tightly.
- **HPF:** 25–35 Hz at 6–12 dB/oct [70]. For a split bass, HPF the original at 60–90 Hz under a sine sub [3]. Dance mid-bass layer HPF at 100 Hz [64].
- **EQ:**
  - Bottom at 50–80 Hz, attack at 700 Hz, snap at 2.5 kHz [72].
  - Dip at 66 Hz or cut 4.5 dB with a shelf at 1 kHz as needed [39].
  - In rock, let the bass own ~60 Hz and the kick ~100 Hz [65]. Shelf at 67 Hz [65].
- **Compressor:** 1176-style, 1 ms attack, 88 ms release, 6–8 dB GR [39]. ML default release 500 ms, 10 ms attack [30].
- **Saturation:** parallel, band-limited to 250 Hz–5 kHz [39].
- **Pan:** centre and mono [25][75].
- **Sidechain:** dynamic dip at ~65 Hz (rock ~102 Hz) when the kick hits [45][65]. EDM: duck to the kick [64][63].

R&B/pop plate: 2.5 s, pre-delay 87 ms (70–180), low cut 180 Hz [55]. Ballads 4–5 s, HPF 200 Hz / LPF 12 kHz [58]. Pre-delay 20–80 ms [59], >30–40 ms [25], or 20–30 ms start [56]. Return filters HPF ~200 Hz / LPF ~5 kHz [25]. EQ esses out before the reverb [38]. Faster tempos get shorter decays [58]. |
| Delay | Synced to tempo [25]. 1/4 and 1/8 note [1][37]; main vocal 1/8 [2]; 1/4 aux [38]. Feedback 20–30% (2–3 repeats), wet 15–20%, return HPF 400 Hz / LPF 8 kHz [37]. Throws on the last word of phrases [61]; 100% wet throw sends [3]. Delay ms = 60000 / BPM × note fraction (math). |
| Sidechain | The vocal ducks instruments 2:1, 30 ms, 250 ms, ≤2–3 dB [63]. The vocal ducks its own reverb and delay returns [63][58]. Over a 2-track beat, the vocal keys a dynamic mid dip [50]. |

### 3.8 Doubles / backing vocals / harmonies
- **Level:** doubles 4–6 dB below the lead (hooks only) [61]. Harmonies 6–9 dB below [61].
- **HPF:** just below the BV's fundamental [49]. Default 120–200 Hz [E, cf. 37 ad-lib HPF 150–200].
- **EQ:**
  - Cut 3–6 dB at the fundamental [49].
  - ~500 Hz: a couple of dB [49].
  - 2–5 kHz: −2 to −3 dB, leaving presence to the lead [49].
  - Narrow cuts at 6–10 kHz if sibilant [49].
- **Compressor:** 4:1, soft knee, ~10 ms attack, longer release, a few dB GR [49].
- **De-ess:** a few dB, split-band [49]. Default de-ess BVs ~2 dB harder than the lead, because stacked esses add up [E].
- **Pan:** doubles centre or slightly wide [61]. Pairs placed with a 0.5–0.7 ms Haas delay rather than hard pans [49]. Layered ad-lib doubles pitched up a minor third and panned hard [37]. Timing must stay within ~20 ms or it reads as slapback [3].
- **Reverb:** shorter pre-delay than the lead so BVs sit behind it [49]. Match the lead's decay length [49].
- **Delay:** 160 ms slapback for thickening [49].
- **Ducking:** none needed. Keep them under the lead with level.

### 3.9 Ad-libs
- **Level:** 6–10 dB below the lead [37].
- **HPF:** 150–200 Hz [37].
- **Pitch:** retune 5–8 ms, faster than the lead [37].
- **Compression:** slightly less than the lead [61].
- **Reverb:** more than the lead, decay 1.2–2.0 s [37][61]. A high shelf down "filters them back" [2].
- **Delay:** more 1/4-note delay than the lead [2]. Sample-delay Haas width [36].
- **Pan:** 15–30% L or R, alternating [37]. Or Haas-pushed to the far sides [36].
- **Saturation:** subtle distortion on doubles and ad-libs in drill [73].
- **Timing:** automate entries on the first beat of a bar [61].

### 3.10 Keys / piano
- **Level:** accent in busy mixes, louder in sparse ones [46]. Default −10 to −14 dB vs vocal [E].
- **HPF:** ~75 Hz, sweeping up until the lowest note changes then backing off 5 Hz. Higher when the piano supports a dense mix [46].
- **EQ:**
  - +1–2 dB wide at 75–200 Hz for warmth [46].
  - Dynamic −1–2 dB at 300–600 Hz [46].
  - Leave 600 Hz–2 kHz alone [46].
  - ±1–2 dB around 4 kHz [46].
  - +9–11 kHz for hammer attack, shelf at 13 kHz, LPF 15–20 kHz [46].
- **Compressor:** 3:1, 5 ms, 50 ms, ~5 dB GR [46].
- **Pan:** stereo spread. In busy mixes, narrow it and offset it opposite the hats [46].
- **Reverb:** 1.5 s, pre-delay 20–30 ms, return filtered at 600 Hz and 6 kHz [46].

### 3.11 Pads
- **Level:** background. Default −14 to −20 dB vs vocal [E].
- **HPF:** high, around 200–430 Hz (430 Hz in the example) [67].
- **EQ:** dynamic cut at 300–500 Hz keyed by the snare [67]. Unmask the vocal band [67]. Carve 1.5–5 kHz when the vocal is present [51][63].
- **Compressor:** light. Default 2:1 at 1–2 dB [E].
- **Width:** wide, but never fold to 0% (it causes phasing). Narrow partially [67].
- **Reverb:** long but filtered. Cut the lows on the reverb [67].
- **Sidechain:** EDM pumps pads and synths from the kick (4:1, fast) [63]. The vocal can duck the music bus by 2–3 dB [63].

### 3.12 Guitars
**Electric**
- HPF ≥150 Hz [47]. Rhythm guitar 80–120 Hz, lead 150–200 Hz [70]. Rock defaults: rhythm 100 Hz, lead 160 Hz [E within 47/70].
- EQ:
  - 250–500 Hz: warmth or clarity [47].
  - +1–2 dB at 2 kHz [47].
  - Dynamic cut at 4 kHz [47].
  - LPF 5–8 kHz [47].
  - Landmarks: fullness 240–500 Hz, presence 1.5–2.5 kHz [72].
- Compression: 3:1 for strummed parts (up to 8:1 for leads), 5 ms attack, 50 ms release [47].
- Pan: double-tracked rhythms hard L/R [47]; single parts off-centre [47]. Country keeps rhythm electrics wide with the centre clear [75].
- Reverb: spring, return filtered at 600 Hz and 6 kHz [47]. Pre-delay 40–100 ms [59].
- Width: imager adds width from 800 Hz to 5 kHz and more above 6 kHz [47]. Chorus slightly wider in rock [65].

**Acoustic**
- HPF ~80 Hz [69].
- EQ: sweep 100–400 Hz for mud, clarity at 1–3 kHz, sparkle at 3–5 kHz, air shelf at 12 kHz [69].
- Compression: 2–4:1, 10–25 ms attack, 50–150 ms release, 1–3 dB GR [69].
- Reverb: small room [69].

### 3.13 Synth leads / melodies
- **Level:** default −6 to −10 dB vs vocal when they play together. Lead synths can be featured in instrumental hooks [E].
- **HPF:** 150–200 Hz [E, cf. lead guitar 150–200 in 70].
- **EQ:** cut 2–5 kHz under the vocal [73]. Use a dynamic dip keyed by the vocal [50][63].
- **Compressor:** ML default for "other" stems is 15 ms attack, 666 ms release [30].
- **Pan:** off-centre or wide. A stereo tremolo can move melodic instruments [36].
- **Sidechain:** EDM ducks them from the kick at 4:1 with fast attack and release [63].
- **Reverb and delay:** tempo-synced delays [25].

### 3.14 FX / risers / impacts
- **HPF:** default 150–200 Hz unless the impact owns the sub [E].
- **Level:** automated. Default peaks ≤ the vocal level [E].
- **Width:** wide.
- **Reverb:** reverse and transition use [3].
- **Ducking:** riser tails duck under the downbeat kick or vocal (2:1, 30 ms, 250 ms, 2–3 dB) [63].

### 3.15 Full instrumental "2-track beat"
- **Level:** a stereo stem, so set the vocal ≈ beat loudness (vocal ≈ sum of all others) [25]. Turn the beat down with clip gain for headroom [50].
- **HPF:** none, or 20–30 Hz on the bus [70].
- **EQ:**
  - Side channel: cut ~250 Hz and ~2 kHz [50].
  - Mid channel: dynamic dip keyed by the vocal, in the 1.5–5 kHz intelligibility band [50][51]. The 2–4 kHz articulation zone matters most [51].
  - Static subtractive cut at 2–5 kHz [73].
  - Check 200–300 Hz for conflicts [50].
- **Dynamics:** a few dB of multiband expansion to restore transients [50]. A side-only transient expander [50].
- **Bus compression:** music-bus compression at 1.5:1 [38].
- **Saturation:** hard-clip low-end saturation for the 808 inside the beat [50].
- **Width:** about 30% extra on the beat bus is acceptable in hip-hop [38]. Leave the mid channel for the vocal [50].
- **Ducking:** the vocal keys the beat by 2–3 dB max [63]. The snare can trigger vocal multiband compression to lock the vocal into the groove [11].
- **Tonal match:** match the vocal's tonal colour to the beat [11]. Match-EQ range ≤12 dB [51].

### 3.16 Room / overhead mics
- **Level:** "just enough to add air and depth" [71].
- **HPF:** 40 Hz [71] to 200–300 Hz [70]. Overheads that only carry cymbals can go higher [70].
- **EQ:** −5 dB at 100–200 Hz, +1–2 dB at ~5 kHz [71].
- **Compression:** 3:1, ~110 ms attack, ~70 ms release [71]. LA-2A-style on room mics [65]. Transient shaper to raise sustain [65].
- **Pan:** overheads spread L/R. Rooms wide [E].
- **Reverb:** room ambience only [65].

### 3.17 Drum bus and parallel drums
- **Drum bus:** SSL-style 4:1, 10 ms attack, 4–6 dB GR [41]. Or a limiter at 5–7 dB GR [71]. HPF the drum bus before compression at 80–100 Hz only when the 808 is on its own bus [70].
- **Parallel ("New York") bus:** ~2.5:1 to max ratio, ≤1 ms attack, 100–300 ms release, very heavy GR (~30 dB) [42][43]. Blend until just audible, then pull down 1–2 dB [43].
- **ML default:** drums 10 ms attack, 180 ms release [30].

## 4. Per-genre profiles

LUFS figures are for the **release master**. Measured values come from charts [19][20][21][22] and engineer practice [36][38][17].

**Recommended product default:**
- Offer "Streaming-optimised" at −14 LUFS and −1 dBTP [13][14].
- Offer "Competitive" at the genre value below with a −1 dBTP ceiling.

Why −1 dBTP for the competitive preset:
- Most charting masters overshoot 0 dBTP [19].
- Spotify asks for −2 dBTP above −14 LUFS [13].
- A −1 dBTP ceiling plus a 4× oversampled limiter is the practical compromise [E].

### 4.1 Hip-hop / rap (boom-bap and mainstream)
- **Vocal vs beat:** vocal loud, dry and upfront [77]. Vocal ≈ beat sum [25]. Default vocal +1 to +2 LU over the instrumental bus [E].
- **Low end:** the kick ducks the 808 or bass [2]. Hip-hop has the most extended lows and the lowest centroid (662 Hz) [27].
- **Tilt:** bright top. Hip-hop has the brightest top end of the genres measured, more than 10 dB brighter than some [27].
- **Space:** dry. Short room or plate, a 1/8 slap or delay on the lead, more on ad-libs [2][37]. Reverb 1.5–2.5 s in the standard hip-hop chain [37] but kept low in level.
- **Width:** hip-hop is the narrowest genre [19]. Keep the low end mono [25].
- **Bus compression:** 2:1, 30 ms, auto release, 2–4 dB [41]. Drums clipped [2].
- **LUFS / TP:** −9 approval level [38]; −9 "conservative" [36]; up to −6 measured [22]. Target −8 to −9 LUFS, −1 dBTP [E].
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Parallel 1176 at 20% [2].
  - Delay throws on single words [3].
  - Stutter edits at 1/16 or 1/32 [3].
  - Clipper before the limiter [2].

### 4.2 Trap
- **Vocal vs beat:** the lead sits on top, with ad-libs 6–10 dB under and panned 15–30% [37].
- **Low end:** a dominant, saturated, tuned 808 [35][36][38]. The kick ducks it by ~10 dB of range [34]. Cut kick sub at 30 Hz [38].
- **Tilt:** bright. Hats aggressive with fast 1176 [36]. 808 fuzz at 3 kHz [38].
- **Space:** short plate 0.6–1.2 s, 15–25% wet, 1/4 or 1/8 delay at 20–30% feedback [37].
- **Width:** narrow low end, wide hats and ad-libs [36][66].
- **Bus compression:** light. Loudness comes from clipping [2][38].
- **LUFS / TP:** −6 to −9 measured or practised [22][36][38]. Target −8 LUFS, −1 dBTP [E].
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Hard tune, retune 0–15 ms [37].
  - Vocal compression 8:1 at 6–10 dB GR [37].
  - 808 saturation with a 48 dB/oct LPF at 6.5 kHz [38].
  - 808 bused and clipped with the drums [2].

### 4.3 Drill (UK/NY)
- **Vocal vs beat:** clear, rhythmic and dark [73]. Cut the beat at 2–5 kHz under the vocal [73].
- **Low end:** sliding 808 glides need mono and saturation (as in trap) [35][36]. Keep the kick tight and short [E].
- **Tilt:** vocal +3–5 kHz with an optional shelf at 10–12 kHz [73].
- **Space:** short plate or room, low-level ping-pong or 1/4 delay [73].
- **Width:** wide percussion and hats [66]. Mono low end.
- **Bus compression:** as trap.
- **LUFS / TP:** default −8 LUFS, −1 dBTP [E, from the trap measurements in 22/36].
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Vocal 3–5:1 compression, fast attack, medium release [73].
  - HPF 80–100 Hz [73].
  - Subtle distortion on doubles and ad-libs [73].

### 4.4 R&B
- **Vocal vs beat:** a lush, featured vocal with stacked harmonies 6–9 dB under the lead [61].
- **Low end:** R&B has a "lighter bottom" than hip-hop [27]. Smooth bass rather than distorted [E].
- **Tilt:** darker than hip-hop. R&B LTAS slope ≈ −1.03, steeper than hip-hop's −0.81 [27].
- **Space:** lush plate at 2.5 s, 87 ms pre-delay, low cut 180 Hz [55]. Ballads up to 4–5 s [58]. Dual reverb: short mono plus long stereo [58].
- **Width:** wide harmonies via Haas 0.5–0.7 ms [49].
- **Bus compression:** gentle, 2:1 at ≤2 dB [41]. Default 1–2 dB [E].
- **LUFS / TP:** −9 to −11 LUFS, −1 dBTP [E, from the dynamic-lane −9 to −11 short-term in 16; only 1 R&B song was in [19]].
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Long pre-delay keeps reverb off the words [55][59].
  - Vocal ducks its reverb [58].
  - Opto second-stage compression [44].

### 4.5 Pop
- **Vocal vs beat:** vocal-forward, ≈ sum of the others [25].
- **Low end:** a punchy kick with bass clarity. Kick/bass dynamic EQ dip of 5–10 dB at ~65 Hz [45].
- **Tilt:** pop slope −0.94 and centroid 868 Hz [27]. Air +2–4 dB on the vocal [37][52].
- **Space:** medium plate and tempo delays. Ballads use longer decays [58].
- **Width:** pop is the widest genre [19].
- **Bus compression:** 2:1, 30 ms, auto release, 2–4 dB [41].
- **LUFS / TP:** chart average −8.3 ±1 [19]. Target −8 to −9 LUFS, −1 dBTP.
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Serial compression [44].
  - Doubles 4–6 dB under on hooks only [61].
  - Chorus lift of 0.5–1 dB [65].

### 4.6 Rock
- **Vocal vs beat:** vocal level per the ISMIR norms (vocal −2.7 LU vs mix, kick −13, snare −17) [26]. The dataset is rock-heavy.
- **Low end:** the bass owns ~60 Hz and the kick ~100 Hz, with a dynamic dip at 102 Hz [65].
- **Tilt:** rock slope −0.98 [27]. The vocal top stays under 10 kHz and cymbals carry the sparkle [65].
- **Space:** rooms and overheads for ambience [65][71]. Guitar pre-delay 40–100 ms [59].
- **Width:** double guitars hard L/R [47]. Chorus slightly wider [65].
- **Bus compression:** 2:1 at 2–4 dB [41]. Drum bus 4:1 at 4–6 dB [41]. Parallel drums [42][43][65].
- **LUFS / TP:** measured −5.1 to −11.9, average −8.1 [21]. Target −8 to −9 LUFS, −1 dBTP.
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Drum bus "squash" blended in [65].
  - Bass parallel distortion [39].
  - Chorus +0.5–1 dB [65].

### 4.7 EDM / dance
- **Vocal vs beat:** the vocal is often slightly lower relative to the drop [E]. Watch vocal stridency at 5–8 kHz [20].
- **Low end:** kick first. Sub mono below 80 Hz, sine sub plus 2–3 layers, mid-bass HPF at 100 Hz [64]. Sidechain kick→bass at 5:1, 4 ms, 60 ms [63].
- **Tilt:** electronic has the flattest slope (−0.75) [27], so it is bright and bass-heavy.
- **Space:** large and tempo-synced. Reverb ducked by the vocal [63].
- **Width:** wide synths, mono lows [64].
- **Bus compression:** 2:1 at 2–4 dB [41]. Kick pumping is part of the sound [20].
- **LUFS / TP:** chart −6.2 to −11.3, average −8.3 [20]. Club −7.5 to −9 [17]. Target −8 LUFS, −1 dBTP. LRA 3–4 LU is acceptable [18].
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Pumping pads and synths keyed from the kick [63].
  - HPF everything non-bass at 100–120 Hz [64].
  - Kick 4:1 with 3–4 ms attack [64].

### 4.8 Afrobeats
- **Vocal vs beat:** the vocal must sit above dense percussion and log drum [61].
- **Low end:** log drum plus 808 or sub. Wide vocal cut at 200–400 Hz to clear them [61].
- **Tilt:** bright vocal. +2–4 kHz and a shelf of +2–4 dB above 10 kHz [61].
- **Space:** plate at 0.8–1.5 s, hooks with 2.5 s, 1/4 delay throws on the last word [61].
- **Width:** wide percussion [66]. Doubles centre or slightly wide [61].
- **Bus compression:** gentle. Default 2:1 at 1–2 dB [E].
- **LUFS / TP:** no measured source. Default −9 LUFS, −1 dBTP [E, near the chart average −8.3 in 19].
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Melodic tuning at retune ~20 ms [61].
  - Harmonies a third and fifth above, 6–9 dB under [61].
  - Ad-libs with more reverb [61].

### 4.9 Lo-fi
- **Vocal vs beat:** vocals (if any) are tucked and dark [62].
- **Low end:** warm and soft. Sidechain pumping is gentle [62].
- **Tilt:** dark. LPF on hats and snare [62]. SP-1200-style 26.04 kHz resampling for grit [76].
- **Space:** subtle reverb and delay [62].
- **Width:** moderate [E].
- **Bus compression:** heavy, "usually over-compressed" [62]. Saturation focused on 100 Hz–10 kHz [76].
- **LUFS / TP:** no source number. Default −12 to −14 LUFS, −1 dBTP [E]. The dynamic lane in [16] is −9 to −11 short-term.
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Tape wow/flutter, vinyl crackle, bit reduction (qualitative) [62].
  - Tempo 70–100 BPM [62].
- **Default numbers [E]:** master LPF 8–12 kHz, sample rate 26 kHz or bit-crush 12-bit at 20–30% wet, wow 0.1–0.3% at 0.5–1 Hz, crackle at −35 to −40 dBFS.

### 4.10 Country / acoustic
- **Vocal vs beat:** vocal centre with a clear centre lane [75].
- **Low end:** mono bass [75]. Moderate low end, with folk the darkest genre measured (slope −1.18, centroid 603 Hz) [27].
- **Tilt:** natural. Watch vocal stridency at ~3.5 kHz [22].
- **Space:** short band reverbs with longer tails on accents [75]. Acoustic guitar in a small room [69].
- **Width:** rhythm electrics wide [75]. Steel and fiddle panned and ridden in fills [75].
- **Bus compression:** light [75]. Acoustic bus 2–4:1 at 1–3 dB [69].
- **LUFS / TP:** measured −8.6 to −9.3 [22]. Target −9 LUFS, −1 dBTP.
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Acoustic HPF at 80 Hz, 12 kHz air shelf [69].
  - Signature instruments up in intros and fills [75].

### 4.11 Reggaeton / Latin
- **Vocal vs beat:** keep the beat loud and uncluttered under rapped sections [74].
- **Low end:** deep sub on quarter notes 1 and 3. HPF sub and kick for balance, and fix kick phase [74]. Remove sub from drum rolls [74].
- **Tilt:** bright and boxy snare. +200–400 Hz on the snare, with high mids pushed [74].
- **Space:** short and gated drums [74]. Vocal space as pop or R&B [55].
- **Width:** detuned snare doubles panned L/R, checked in mono [74].
- **Bus compression:** default 2:1 at 2–3 dB [41].
- **LUFS / TP:** no measured source. Default −8 LUFS, −1 dBTP [E].
- **Signature tricks:**
  - Dembow pattern with snares on 16th positions 4, 7, 12 and 15 [74].
  - Short percussion [74].

## 5. Mix bus and master rules

1. **Glue compression**
   - 2:1, 30 ms attack, auto release (or 100–300 ms), 2–4 dB GR. Past 4–5 dB sounds over-compressed [41]. Typical pro value is ~3 dB [25].
   - Vari-mu-style glue is an option [11].
   - On 808-heavy mixes avoid slow wideband compression that the 808 pumps. Use a sidechain HPF on the detector (~100 Hz [E]) [38][41].
2. **Clip before limit.** A soft clipper takes 1–3 dB of peaks before the limiter [2][36][38].
   - The limiter "knocks off a dB here or there" [1].
   - Up to ~6 dB of peak limiting is generally unnoticeable [25].
   - Default: clipper 1–2 dB, limiter ≤3–4 dB GR [E].
3. **Limiter**
   - Ceiling −1 dBTP [13][14][18].
   - If the master is louder than −14 LUFS, Spotify recommends −2 dBTP [13]. Masters aiming for −2 dBTP should target about −8 LUFS short-term rather than −6 [16].
   - Reality check: most charting masters sit above 0 dBTP [19].
4. **Loudness targets**
   - Spotify −14 (Loud −11, Quiet −19) [13]. Apple −16, YouTube −14 (turns down only), Amazon and TIDAL −14, Deezer −15 [14][15].
   - Chart averages: −8.3 ±1 LUFS, with short-term max 2–4 LU above integrated [19].
   - A −8 LUFS master will be turned down about 6 dB on Spotify [13]. Loudness buys density, not volume.
   - LRA: 6–12 LU is healthy, 3–4 LU acceptable for loop-based dance [18]. Half of 2024 charting songs fell between 3.7 and 6.8 LU [19].
5. **Tonal balance target (long-term average spectrum)**
   - Overall shape: about −5 dB/octave from 100 Hz to 4 kHz, steeper roll-off above, low cut around 60 Hz [27]. Pink-noise-like with extra roll-off at both ends [25].
   - TBC bands: <250 Hz, 250 Hz–2 kHz, 2–8 kHz, >8 kHz. Bump below 250 Hz, broadly flat 250 Hz–8 kHz on the TBC display, steep roll-off above 8 kHz [31].
   - Genre tilt from flattest (brightest) to steepest (darkest): electronic −0.75, disco −0.80, hip-hop −0.81, pop −0.94, rock −0.98, soul −1.03, R&B −1.03, folk −1.18, jazz −1.29 [27].
   - Mix centroid ≈ 2.48 ±0.52 kHz in the ISMIR stem study [26]. This uses a different method from the genre centroids in [27], so don't mix the two.
   - Even 1 dB missing at the top sounds dull [25].
6. **Stereo**
   - Low end mono via M/S: HPF the side channel at ~80–120 Hz [40][64][25].
   - L/R balance within 0.8 dB [25].
   - Side channel ~8.5 LU below mid on average [19].
   - Pan law −3 dB equal-power [24].
7. **Master HPF.** Subtle 20–30 Hz at 6–12 dB/oct, linear-phase if available [70].

## 6. Using a reference track

**What engineers do**
- Match loudness first. Turn the reference down to your mix's level, never the reverse. Use short-term LU and switch in about 5-second chunks, chorus against chorus [32].
- Match loudness before any A/B [2].
- Monitor around 85 dB SPL [32].
- Compare one relationship at a time [32]:
  - Tonal balance per band.
  - Kick and bass relationship.
  - Vocal-to-FX ratio (not absolute vocal level).
  - Width and correlation.
  - Loudness-history dynamics.
- A/B against commercial masters with a level-matched switcher [38].

**What is legitimately measurable (the features Diff-MST uses to match references [29])**
1. Integrated, short-term and momentary LUFS, plus LRA (EBU R128).
2. Bark or 1/3-octave long-term spectrum. Compare after loudness normalization, using the TBC band split <250 / 250–2k / 2–8k / >8k [31].
3. RMS and crest factor (PLR) per section [29].
4. Stereo width (side/mid energy ratio, per band) and L/R imbalance [29][19].
5. Low-end mono-ness: correlation below 120 Hz [E].
6. Tempo and key, for delay times and 808 tuning [35].

**Rules for our tool**
- The reference is a mastered file. Compare our post-limiter output to it after normalizing both to the same LUFS [32].
- Apply only a **smoothed** match-EQ difference (1/3-octave or wider) on the mix bus. Cap it at ±3 dB per band [24][E], and keep the match-EQ range ≤12 dB total [51].
- Copy loudness and width targets, not the reference's exact EQ curve. Reference-matching systems can homogenize style and miss masking [29].
- Static parameters are a known limitation of reference matching [29]. Keep per-section automation (vocal riding, chorus lift) separate.
- **Practical constraint [E]:** Spotify, Apple and YouTube links are DRM-protected or CORS-blocked in the browser. Ask the user to upload a local audio file of the reference.
  - Analyze it in memory only, and never redistribute it.
  - From a link alone, use metadata (genre, BPM, key) to pick the genre profile.

## 7. Common mistakes and numeric guardrails

| Mistake | Guardrail |
|---|---|
| Over-EQing | Default EQ moves ±3 dB [24]. Air boosts are "a few dB" [52]. Any single boost over +6 dB is a red flag [E]. Cut with a higher Q than you boost [25]. |
| Blanket HPF thinning everything | Blanket HPF was rejected by experts [25]. HPF only tracks with no useful lows, sweeping up until the sound changes then backing off 5–10 Hz [45][46]. Drop the HPF if the track's energy below the cutoff is under −30 dB relative to its peak band [E]. |
| Kick and 808 fighting | Sidechain or dynamic EQ for 5–10 dB of reduction at the kick fundamental [45][34]. Tune the 808 [35]. Check polarity [38]. |
| Stereo low end | Mono below 80–120 Hz [64][40]. Content below 200 Hz centred [25]. |
| Vocal buried by the beat | Vocal ≈ loudness of everything else combined [25]. Duck the beat 2–3 dB in 1.5–5 kHz while the vocal sings [50][51][63]. |
| Vocal-band masking | Keep the beat's 2–4 kHz energy ≤ the vocal's in that band when sung [E, from 51/50]. Prefer a dynamic dip over a static cut [50]. |
| Too much reverb (the top auto-mix failure [28]) | Reverb return ≈ −9 LU vs dry [25]. Trap wet 15–25% [37]. Filter returns at 200 Hz–5 kHz [25]. Never send sub to reverb [25]. |
| Esses in reverb and delay | De-ess before sends, cut the reverb's input highs [38]. Total de-esser reduction 4–6 dB max [37]. |
| Over-compressing the vocal | Stage 1 peaks 5–12 dB [44]. Stage 2 ≤3 dB [44]. Total average GR ≤ ~8–10 dB [E, from 37/48]. |
| Over-compressing the mix bus | ≤4 dB GR [41]. ~3 dB typical [25]. |
| Clipping and inter-sample peaks | Leave about 6 dB of mix headroom [33]. Vocal peaks ≤ −6 dBFS pre-chain [37]. Master ceiling −1 dBTP [13]. |
| Chasing loudness | Platforms turn loud masters down (Spotify −14 [13], YouTube turns down only [15]). Limiting past ~6 dB GR is unnecessary [25]. |
| Comparing at unequal loudness | Always loudness-match before deciding [2][32]. |
| Ad-libs fighting the lead | Ad-libs 6–10 dB under [37]. Doubles 4–6 dB under [61]. BV timing within ~20 ms of the lead [3]. |
| Static mix | Automate: chorus +0.5–1 dB [65], vocal riding [54], delay throws [61]. |
| Phase cancellation between layers | Check correlation. Flip polarity or offset by a few ms [64][38]. Folding pads to 0% width causes phasing [67]. |

## 7. The arrangement: sessions, doubles, sections and build-ups (added 2026-11-05)

What the mix does when it can see the session and the song's shape. Numbers in the code: `timeline.js`, `rules.js` (`arrange` per genre), the `double` role.

### 7.1 Reading the session
- Pro Tools (`.ptx`, `.ptf`), REAPER (`.rpp`, plain text: TRACK, ITEM with POSITION/LENGTH/SOFFS/MUTE and SOURCE FILE) and Ableton Live (`.als`, gzipped XML: AudioTrack → ArrangerAutomation → AudioClip, placed in beats at the master tempo; unwarped clips keep their file offset in seconds, warped ones are read best-effort) are read. A session file on its own is read and shown; the audio can follow in another drop. Of several session files the newest that places audio wins, since a DAW's auto-backup is often ahead of the main file.
- A Pro Tools session (`.ptx`) carries what exported stems lose: where every recording sits (a clip can play several times), how clips are trimmed, which files are on no track at all (unused takes), and the track names. Chucksi reads it with its own parser written from the publicly understood layout [89]; mutes, fades, volume and plug-ins are not read, the engine makes those calls.
- Track names give roles: "Main" (lead; A and B alternate lines), "Dub L / Dub R" (doubles, panned by the name), "Libbs" (ad-libs), "Instrumental" (the beat). Buses, subs, prints and masters are not recordings.
- A clip placed more than once is a hook: it marks a chorus.

### 7.2 Doubles
- Level 9 dB under the lead (sources range 10–20 dB under for felt-not-heard [78][79], 4–6 under for hooks [61]); +2 to +3 dB in choruses, −1.5 to −3 in verses [85][82][88].
- HPF 160 Hz (above the lead's), −3 dB at 300 Hz, −1.5 dB at 3.5 kHz so presence stays with the lead [49], a −2 dB shelf at 10 kHz; de-essed 9 dB, harder than the lead, so only one "s" is heard [80]; 4:1 with 5 dB of reduction; pan hard by name (0.9, further doubles on the same side at 0.75 and 0.6) [88][82]; room, no delay.
- Tightening: each phrase of a double is cross-correlated (7 ms smoothed 1 kHz envelopes) against the lead within ±60 ms and lifted into place with 3 ms fades; no stretching [79][80]. On Falling, doubles sat 20–60 ms off the lead in places.
- Made doubles: when choruses have a lone lead, two copies pitched ∓6 cents, 10 and 20 ms late, hard left and right, 9 dB under, in the choruses only [78][80][87].

### 7.3 Clean-up
- A gate curve per vocal strip: opens when the stem is 12 dB over its own noise floor, holds 150 ms, closes over 250 ms to −18 dB (doubles, ad-libs) or −9 dB (lead, whose breaths belong to the take). Stems that never stop (pads) are left alone.

### 7.4 Sections
- Per bar: how many vocal stems are active (a stem counts when it plays a quarter of the bar), whether a lead sings, and the music's level. A bar is a chorus when its stack is at least three voices and about half the song's biggest stack, or when a hook clip plays there; a verse when a lead sings without the stack; a break otherwise. A single bar never makes a section. With one voice throughout, the louder bars are the chorus.

### 7.5 The build-up into a chorus [83][84][85][86]
- A high-pass on the music sweeps from 25 Hz to the genre's top (250 Hz rock, 320 pop, 400–450 hip-hop/trap/drill, 600 EDM) over the last two bars, then is gone at the downbeat.
- The beat drops for the last two beats (one in R&B, lo-fi and country; a bar in EDM), 40 ms edges; the vocal carries the gap.
- The dry punch: every vocal effect send off for the last beat (half a beat in gentle genres), back at the downbeat.
- The throw: the lead's reverb send +8 dB (4–10 by genre) over the last bar up to the dry beat, so the last words ring into the gap.
- Lifts with 300 ms ramps: chorus lead +1 to +1.5 dB, doubles +2 to +3 dB, ad-libs +1; verse lead reverb +2 to +4 dB, doubles tucked.
- The first section never gets a build-up (nothing precedes it).

## 9. The lead bus in bands, air, and the doubler (added 2026-11-05, evening)

Four vocal-chain videos [97]–[100], a written Drake chain [101] and three free REAPER templates [105] were read for this round. What they add to section 8:

| They do | Chucksi now does |
| --- | --- |
| **Multiband compression on the vocal** (Pro-MB [98]; the Maserati tutorial in the same playlist [100]); ReaXcomp on the master [105] | The lead bus runs in three bands through Linkwitz-Riley crossovers (220 Hz and 4.5 kHz, which sum flat): the lows and the highs get their own compressors (3:1 at −30 and 2.5:1 at −34 dBFS, 2 ms attack on the top), the mids get the glue. | **Pitch correction as texture** [100] | The Tune macro (−3 off … +3 fastest) already does this; the Leslie Brathwaite note that a *lower* retune speed reads as the modern robotic sound matches the genre table (trap 20 ms, drill 30 ms). |

Two fixes found while measuring: the parallel distortion path under the lead had one compressor fewer than the lead and so landed about 6 ms early (a pre-echo); it now passes a neutral compressor. And the browser's low-pass and high-pass filters take Q in decibels, not as a ratio, so every 0.707 and the 0.54 / 1.31 Butterworth pair were small resonant bumps at their cutoffs; the filter helper now converts, which flattened the crossovers from ±5 dB to ±0.6 dB and tidied the strips' high-pass.

**One song file:** the split stems take the whole path above: the vocal stem is tuned to the song's key, the drums get punch and parallel compression, the arrangement reads sections and builds up into choruses, a lone lead gets made doubles on the hook bus (now with the doubler), and the lead bus runs in bands with air. The one thing a split cannot give is separate doubles and ad-libs: they are inside the one vocal stem, so the stack is made rather than found.

Candidates for the next round, by expected gain: Mel-Band RoFormer for the split on WebGPU machines (+2 dB vocal SDR [103]); a vocal "clean" step (DeepFilterNet3 [104], or a spectral gate from the noise floor the analysis already measures) ahead of the chain, which would also tidy split-stem bleed; a soothe-style dynamic resonance cut (per-band detectors on 2–6 kHz); harmonies from the retune pitch track; master-bus sub weight and stereo enhancer from the REAPER master chain [105].
