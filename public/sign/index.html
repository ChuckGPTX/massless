How it stays private

The key is in the link. When the sender creates an envelope, their browser makes a random 256-bit key, encrypts the PDF, the field layout and the filled-in details with AES-GCM, and uploads only the ciphertext. The key travels after the # in the link, a part browsers never send to a server.

What Chucksi sees: encrypted blobs, their sizes, SHA-256 fingerprints, how many signers there are and in what order, and when each step happened, from which IP address and browser. What it never sees: the document's text, names and emails, anything typed into fields, signatures, the message or decline reasons.

Proof. Each step is logged with the server's time, IP and user agent. When everyone has signed, a browser with the key builds the final PDF and a Certificate of Completion. Chucksi signs the audit trail with an ECDSA P-256 key and records the final file's fingerprint, so Verify can prove a file is unchanged.

Retention. Unsigned envelopes are deleted after 30 days, completed ones after 90. The sender can delete any time.

Not for every document. U.S. e-signature law (the ESIGN Act and UETA) does not cover wills, codicils and testamentary trusts; adoption, divorce and other family-law matters; court orders, notices and official court papers; notices of utility shutoff, default, foreclosure, eviction or repossession of a primary residence; cancellation of health or life insurance; product recall notices; and papers that must travel with hazardous materials. Deeds for recording, notarized or witnessed documents and some other instruments may need wet ink, a notary or a set process. Check before you send.
