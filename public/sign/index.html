How it stays private

The key is in the link. When you create an envelope, the document, its fields and the filled-in details are encrypted before they are sent. Only people with the link can open them.

What Chucksi sees: encrypted files, their sizes and fingerprints, how many signers there are and in what order, and when each step happened, from which IP address and browser. What it never sees: the document's text, names and emails, anything typed into fields, signatures, the message or decline reasons.

Proof. Each step is logged with the time, IP address and browser. When everyone has signed, the final PDF gets a Certificate of Completion, and Chucksi seals the audit trail so Verify can prove a file is unchanged.

Retention. Unsigned envelopes are deleted after 30 days, completed ones after 90. The sender can delete any time.

Not for every document. U.S. e-signature law (the ESIGN Act and UETA) does not cover wills, codicils and testamentary trusts; adoption, divorce and other family-law matters; court orders, notices and official court papers; notices of utility shutoff, default, foreclosure, eviction or repossession of a primary residence; cancellation of health or life insurance; product recall notices; and papers that must travel with hazardous materials. Deeds for recording, notarized or witnessed documents and some other instruments may need wet ink, a notary or a set process. Check before you send.
